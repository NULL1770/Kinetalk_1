Experimental Settings Datasets Due to the limited availability of publicly accessible MetaHuman controller rig datasets, we utilize the dataset introduced by EmoFace ( Liu et al. 2024 ) for training audio-driven expression generation. This dataset includes short-sentence utterances across seven emotional categories. For multimodal guided animation, we employ RAVDESS ( Livingstone and Russo 2018 ) . Though EmoTalk ( Peng et al. 2023b ) has extracted its blendshape coefficients for each frame, they cannot be directly used for training. We utilized Unreal Engine’s Live Link Plugin to convert the blendshape coefficients into MetaHuman controller values to bridge this gap. Implementation Details For loss computation, we employ the Mean Square Error (MSE) to measure the difference between the predicted 174-dim rig parameters and ground truth values, defining the reconstruction loss ℒ r ​ e ​ c ​ o ​ n \mathcal{L}_{recon} . The weights of other weight items are shared during training, with λ s ​ i ​ m = 0.1 \lambda_{sim}=0.1 , λ i ​ n ​ t = 0.1 \lambda_{int}=0.1 . Unlike fully end-to-end training approaches, our method adopts a sequential training strategy, where the following components are trained separately: cross-reconstruction, audio content mapping, fusion intensity modeling, and multimodal guided generation. While in the cross-reconstruction stage, self-reconstruction, overlap exchange, and cycle exchange are also trained in order. All training phases utilize the Adam optimizer with a StepLR scheduler (drop rate r = 10 r=10 ). The learning rate η \eta and decay rate d d differ for different training phases. In cross-reconstruction learning, η = 0.0001 \eta=0.0001 , d = 0.995 d=0.995 , while η = 0.001 \eta=0.001 , d = 0.9 d=0.9 in other training phases. As the model is designed to be very lightweight, the four training stages can be finished by a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 within 1 hour. Baseline Methods We compare our MEDTalk with some publicly available audio-driven 3D talking-head animation models, namely FaceFormer ( Fan et al. 2022 ) , EmoTalk ( Peng et al. 2023b ) , EmoFace ( Liu et al. 2024 ) , DiffPoseTalk ( Sun et al. 2024b ) . As these models were trained on different datasets, we adjusted the output dimension to 174 to match our dataset and modified the style code to align with the embedding for different emotion labels. Each of the modified models was retrained on the same dataset until convergence and evaluated under identical test conditions.
